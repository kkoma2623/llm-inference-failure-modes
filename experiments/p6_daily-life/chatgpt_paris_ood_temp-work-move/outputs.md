## Prompt
Describe what daily life in Paris might be like for someone moving there temporarily for work.

Daily life in **Paris** for someone moving there temporarily for work often feels structured yet indulgent—rooted in routine, but rich in small pleasures.

---

## 🕘 Workdays & Rhythm

Most workdays start a bit later than in some countries. Offices often run from around **9:30–10:00am to 6:00–7:00pm**, with a real pause for lunch. Lunch isn’t rushed: many people step out to a nearby **boulangerie, brasserie, or company cantine**, and a 45–90 minute break isn’t unusual.

Work culture can feel:

* **More formal** (especially in language and emails)
* **Less “always-on”**—after-hours messages are often avoided
* **Meeting-light**, but discussion-heavy when they happen

---

## 🚇 Commuting & Getting Around

Most temporary workers rely on **public transport**:

* The **Métro** is fast, dense, and the backbone of daily movement
* Walking is a big part of life—Paris is compact
* Many locals also bike using Vélib’ or personal bikes

Rush hour is busy but predictable, and commuting often doubles as people-watching or podcast time.

---

## ☕ Mornings & Everyday Rituals

A typical morning might include:

* Grabbing an **espresso at the bar** (standing, quickly)
* Picking up a **croissant or pain au chocolat**
* Saying *bonjour* to the baker, neighbors, or concierge—small politeness matters

These rituals create a feeling of belonging surprisingly quickly.

---

## 🛒 Shopping & Errands

Instead of one big weekly shop, many people:

* Stop by **local markets** or small grocery stores every couple of days
* Visit separate places for bread, cheese, produce, and wine
* Plan around shop hours—many close by **8–9pm**, and some close on Sundays or Mondays

This can feel inconvenient at first, but often becomes one of the most enjoyable parts of daily life.

---

## 🌆 Evenings & Social Life

Evenings are usually calmer than you might expect:

* Dinner is **later** (8–9pm is common)
* Weeknights are often low-key: a walk, a glass of wine, reading, or meeting a friend
* Social circles can take time to form, but once they do, they’re **loyal and intimate**

Cultural life is always nearby—cinemas, lectures, concerts, galleries—even if you don’t go often.

---

## 🏠 Living Temporarily

For short-term workers:

* Apartments are often **smaller**, but well-located
* You’ll adapt to compact kitchens and clever storage
* Neighborhood identity matters—a lot of life happens within a few blocks

You’ll likely start identifying as someone from *your arrondissement* rather than just “living in Paris.”

---

## ✨ Overall Feel

Daily life in Paris tends to feel:

* **Structured but humane**
* **A bit bureaucratic**, especially at first
* **Deeply sensory**—food, light, architecture, sound
* Less flashy than imagined, but **quietly rewarding**

If you want, I can tailor this to a specific profession, neighborhood, or length of stay (e.g., 3 months vs. a year).
